## 4. Missing-premise and circularity checks

No circular step was found in the *stated adopted P_0* argument. The following tempting substitutions would introduce missing premises:

- Inferring delta_0(A)>=1 from bare MP_0; integer coefficients only upgrade an already strict central defect, and MP_0 permits zero at some parameters.
- Declaring N=1 Robin blocks strict for arbitrary rho, N=2 prefixes strict, or the SHORT reverse r=-1 child anchor strict under MP_0. The last two need optional MP_01. The actual core consumers have separate lower index bounds and avoid this loss.
- Certifying a generic SHORT seed e'=f_0 via future short moves. Index zero has no seed cone premise; this would assume a future gate to prove its prerequisite.
- Treating y as a cone-preserving multiplier. Every needed smoothed origin conclusion uses its separate y packet or an independently proved factorization.
- Dropping the signed fixed-origin anchor a_O-a_X from E_j, or presuming positivity of a source/residual split. The previous exact -2504 residual is a genuine obstruction to that sourcewise argument.
- Upgrading Q+D<=D to strictness on the longer Q+D support. R(Q+D,D)=R(Q,D) vanishes in the extra support; LONG must acquire the missing strictness elsewhere.
- Carrying old strict reverse-anchor high-band or positive uniform-eta conclusions into P_0. Those older stronger results need their actual strict premises, and are not available merely because their algebraic formulas survive.

The central-flag proof is not circular: it uses parent origin certificates to produce the outgoing v, never the child's current packet. New ordinary origins store the already flagged old current trace; retention leaves a stored trace unchanged. This is conditional induction of one subsystem while complete state induction remains open.
